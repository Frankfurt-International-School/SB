# PE Fundamental Skill Ladders — Grades 6 to 8

**Seven technique-gated skill ladders. One developmental scale (6 levels) used across all three grades; the grade band sets where "meeting expectations" sits. Built to be set up and run by students, verified by a peer against one objective rule, and signed off by the teacher.**

## The dividing line: pattern = skill, output = fitness

The research settles a question that is genuinely muddled in the field ([Utesch et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-019-01068-y) flag the "overlap in content" directly): a movement is a **skill** when we score the **pattern / control quality** (process assessment — *how* it is done), and a **fitness** item when we score the **output** — a distance, a time, a count ([Capio et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.3109/09638288.2011.577502)). The same movement can be either depending on what we measure.

So this document holds only the **seven skills we judge on the movement pattern**. Anything scored as a number lives on the **fitness** side:

- **Skills (this document — climb levels, judged on the pattern):** throw, catch, strike, dribble, kick (object control) · jump & land, core stability (body control).
- **Fitness (a score you beat):** mile (CV) · push-ups, grip (strength) · standing broad jump (power) · 30 m (speed) · **Illinois agility** (change-of-direction time) · **stork balance** (losses in 60 s).

*Balance and agility sit in fitness because every validated balance/agility test is an **output** (a count, a time, a distance) — there is no process-based balance test, because balance has no "movement pattern" to score. Balance is measured as the **stork stand, counting losses in 60 s** (Modified Flamingo scoring — ICC 0.84–0.98, [García-Liñeira et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbmt.2023.05.006)); the timed hold is the least reliable Eurofit item and is not used ([Tsigilis et al., 2002](https://doi.org/10.2466/pms.2002.95.3f.1295)). Both live on the fitness recording sheet.*

## The design, in one place

- **One concrete task per level. No choices.** Every student does the identical task, so scores are comparable across kids, classes, years.
- **No partner variance.** Difficulty is set by fixed things only — a marked distance, a fixed target size, a rep count, a **clock**, or a **self-feed** (throw at a wall, hit off a tee, roll your own ball). A partner never sets the difficulty. *One deliberate exception: the top strike rungs use a **standardised live toss** (see Strike) — game realism needs a moving ball, so the toss is tightly defined and a bad feed is voided, never counted against the striker.*
- **Technique is gated at the base, physics-enforced at the top.**
  - **L1 = the movement pattern, gated by ONE binary cue** (e.g. throw = steps with the opposite foot). One yes/no cue is reliable to judge; a checklist is not.
  - **L2 = pattern + a basic outcome.**
  - **L3–L6 = escalating fixed constraint** (distance / target size / count / clock / load) where the constraint itself *demands* good form, so the outcome becomes an honest proxy.
- **Self → Peer → Teacher.** Student self-rates → peer verifies the one objective rule and initials → teacher gives the final holistic ✔. A level is only **banked** once teacher-cleared. (Research: peers/teachers judge an *overall* level reliably, but not multi-point criteria live — so we ask them for one binary check plus a holistic sign-off, never a checklist.)

### One scale, three grades

The **level is the same** in every grade. What changes is the **grade band** — where "proficient / meeting expectations" sits:

| Grade | "Proficient" band (default — calibrate per skill after cycle 1) |
|---|---|
| **G6** | Levels **2–3** |
| **G7** | Levels **3–4** |
| **G8** | Levels **4–5** |

Levels 1 and 6 are the floor and stretch, so no one hits a floor or a ceiling. Reporting: a student's level, read against their grade band, gives the 1–7 grade — the **same L4 reads as 7 (excellent) for a G6, 5 (good) for a G7, 4 (satisfactory) for a G8.**

> **Numbers are v1.** Distances, counts and grade bands are sensible, research-anchored starting points. Calibrate them against your kids after the first cycle — the *architecture* is fixed, the *numbers* are yours to tune.

## OBJECT-CONTROL SKILLS

### 1 · THROW 🎯
**Setup:** the dividing curtain with two taped zones (a 2×2 m zone and a 1 m target); floor distance marks at 5, 6, 8, 10, 12 m; 5 tennis balls. *Balls hit the curtain and drop straight down — no backstop or ball-chase needed.*
**L1 form gate (binary):** *steps forward with the opposite foot to the throwing arm.*
| Level | Task | Pass rule |
|---|---|---|
| **L1** | Overhand throw at the curtain from 5 m | **5/5** with the opposite-foot step (contact anywhere) |
| **L2** | Overhand into the 2×2 m zone from 6 m | **4/5** in, step correct |
| **L3** | Overhand into the 1 m target from 8 m | **3/5** in |
| **L4** | 1 m target from 10 m | **3/5** in |
| **L5** | 1 m target from 10 m, **5 throws in 25 s** | **4/5** in |
| **L6** | 1 m target from 12 m, 5 in 25 s | **4/5** in |

### 2 · CATCH 🧤
**Setup:** a **solid wall** (needs the rebound); one large ball and one tennis ball; a fixed 3 m mark. *Self-fed: you throw at the wall and catch the rebound — a wall returns the ball the same way every time.*
**L1 form gate (binary):** *catches with the hands only — not trapped against the body/chest.*
| Level | Task (throw at wall from 3 m, catch the rebound) | Pass rule |
|---|---|---|
| **L1** | Large ball, two hands | **4/5** caught, hands only |
| **L2** | Large ball, two hands | **5/5** caught, hands only |
| **L3** | Tennis ball, two hands | **4/5** caught |
| **L4** | Tennis ball, two hands | **5/6** caught |
| **L5** | Tennis ball, **one hand** (dominant) | **4/6** caught |
| **L6** | Tennis ball, **one hand** (3 each hand) | **5/6** caught |

### 3 · STRIKE 🏏
**Setup:** batting tee, bat, **wiffle balls**, cones making **gates** (3 m-wide and 2 m-wide) set downrange, a marked **tosser's spot 3 m to the side**, floor marks at 5/6/8 m, the **curtain as the backstop**. *Lower rungs are tee-fed (no variance, judges the swing); the top three add a **live tossed ball** — the real striking skill.*
**L1 form gate (binary):** *side-on stance, hands together, steps into the ball.*
**Fair-toss rule (L4–L6):** the tosser stands **to the side** (out of the swing arc), underhand, a gentle **waist-high arc from 3 m**. A toss outside a waist-high strike zone is a **re-toss** — a bad feed is never scored against the striker. (This is why a light **wiffle ball** is used: slow, safe, forgiving of an imperfect toss, and it won't carry dangerously in a shared gym.)
| Level | Task | Pass rule |
|---|---|---|
| **L1** | Strike **off the tee**, ball travels forward past a 5 m line | **5/5**, side-on + step |
| **L2** | Off the tee, **through a 3 m-wide gate** from 6 m | **4/5** through |
| **L3** | Off the tee, **through a 2 m gate** from 8 m | **3/5** through |
| **L4** | **Tossed wiffle ball** (partner, underhand, from 3 m to the side) — strike it forward past the 5 m line | **4/5** clean contact, forward |
| **L5** | Strike the **tossed ball through a 3 m gate** | **3/5** through |
| **L6** | Strike the **tossed ball through a 2 m gate** | **3/5** through |

### 4 · DRIBBLE ⛹️
*Default: hand-dribble (basketball). Foot-dribble version uses the same structure — state which per unit. Anchored on the TGMD ball-control criteria (push at hip height, one hand, keep control); there is no separately validated slalom test, so difficulty is set by **objective constraints** — cone spacing, hand, head-up — never by "speed," which a peer cannot judge reliably. Want a speed metric? Time the L3 slalom for a personal best, but **control is always the pass, never the clock.***
**Setup:** a 10 m lane; a 5-cone slalom (cones 1.5 m apart; a tight version at 1 m); a ball each; a partner who holds up a number for the head-up levels.
**L1 form gate (binary):** *ball is pushed (not slapped) and stays within arm's reach (≈1 m) — controlled, no chase.*
| Level | Task | Pass rule |
|---|---|---|
| **L1** | On the spot: **10 controlled bounces**, one hand at hip height (push, don't slap) | 10 in control, no loss |
| **L2** | Dribble 10 m straight, **dominant hand** | Ball within 1 m the whole way, no loss |
| **L3** | 5-cone slalom (1.5 m apart), **dominant hand** | Complete, no loss |
| **L4** | 5-cone slalom, **weak hand** | Complete, no loss |
| **L5** | 5-cone slalom, **head up** — call the number your partner holds up | Complete + number, no loss |
| **L6** | **Tight (1 m) slalom, switch hands at each cone, head up** | Complete + number, no loss |

### 5 · KICK ⚽
**Setup:** balls; a 3 m goal and a 2 m goal (cones); a 1 m target; floor marks at 6, 8, 10 m.
**L1 form gate (binary):** *plant foot placed beside the ball; contact with the instep (laces).*
| Level | Task | Pass rule |
|---|---|---|
| **L1** | Kick a stationary ball into a 3 m goal from 6 m | **5/5**, plant foot + instep |
| **L2** | Stationary ball into a 2 m goal from 8 m | **4/5** in |
| **L3** | Stationary ball into the 1 m target from 8 m | **3/5** in |
| **L4** | 1 m target from 10 m | **3/5** in |
| **L5** | **Moving ball** (self-roll 2 steps ahead, then strike) into the 1 m target from 8 m | **3/5** in |
| **L6** | Moving ball into the 1 m target from 10 m | **3/5** in |

## BODY-CONTROL SKILLS

### 6 · JUMP & LAND ⬆️
*This ladder assesses the **landing** (deceleration control), not how far/high you jump — jump power is the standing broad jump on the fitness side. Built on the Landing Error Scoring System (drop-vertical-jump off a ~30 cm box, [Padua et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.4085/1062-6050-50.1.10)) and the repeated-landing logic of the Tuck Jump Assessment ([Herrington et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ptsp.2012.05.005)). A live, holistic landing call is reliable — LESS-RT ICC .72–.81 ([Padua et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1123/jsr.20.2.145)) and excellent total-score reliability even with non-expert raters ([Everard et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1123/jsr.2018-0387)) — so we ask for one binary cue plus a holistic sign-off, never a live checklist.*
**Setup:** floor space; a landing line; one **~30 cm box**.
**SAFETY GATE — applies at *every* level (this is the pass condition):** *knees bend to absorb, knees track over the toes (not caving inward), stuck with no extra step.* An unsafe landing (stiff/straight legs, knees caving in, a stumble or step) does **not** pass at any level. Peer checks "soft and stuck, no step"; **teacher watches for the knee-cave at sign-off.**
> **No skipping on this station, and teacher-watched at the top.** Unlike the other skills, a student may **not** skip levels here — each rung earns the next, so no one jumps straight to a demanding landing they can't yet control. The box and single-leg rungs (L2, L3, L5, L6) are **teacher-watched, not peer-signed.** *The one-foot drop off a box has been deliberately removed — a single-leg landing from height is the highest-risk movement in this set and the worst one for a kid to skip to; the ladder now tops out at a **repeated single-leg hop-and-stick on the floor**, which tests the same single-leg control without the drop from height.*
| Level | Task | Pass rule (all require a soft, stuck landing) |
|---|---|---|
| **L1** | Small two-foot jump straight up on the floor, land and stick | Soft stick, **held 2 s** |
| **L2** | Two-foot **drop off the 30 cm box**, land and stick *(teacher-watched)* | Soft stick, held |
| **L3** | **Drop-vertical-jump** — drop off the box, land, immediately spring straight up, stick the second landing *(the LESS task; teacher-watched)* | Both landings soft, second one stuck |
| **L4** | **Three two-foot jumps forward in a row**, stick the last one | All soft, last one stuck |
| **L5** | **One-foot** hop forward, land and stick on that leg — each leg *(teacher-watched)* | Soft stick, held, each leg |
| **L6** | **Three one-foot hops forward on the same leg**, stick the last — each leg *(teacher-watched)* | All soft, last one stuck |

### 7 · CORE STABILITY 🧱
**Setup:** mats. *Anti-movement — holding position, not reps to failure. The upper levels use the Functional Movement Screen's Trunk Stability Push-Up (TSPU): press up keeping the body one rigid plank; the hand position is the load dial.*
**Form gate (binary, throughout):** *the body holds/moves as ONE rigid unit — hips level, low back flat, no sag and no hip-lag.* **Judge the control, not the clock** — the hold times below are just the vehicle; a wobbly hip disqualifies however long they last.
| Level | Task | Pass rule |
|---|---|---|
| **L1** | Forearm plank | Held **20 s**, hips level |
| **L2** | Forearm plank | Held **40 s**, hips level |
| **L3** | **Plank shoulder-taps** — high plank, tap the opposite shoulder | **20 taps** (10 each side), **no hip sway** |
| **L4** | **TSPU, thumbs at collarbone** — press up moving as one rigid unit | **3 reps**, chest and hips rise together (no hip lag) |
| **L5** | **TSPU, thumbs at chin** (more load) | **3 reps**, rigid unit |
| **L6** | **TSPU, thumbs at forehead** (hardest) | **3 reps**, rigid unit |

## How students run a skill station

1. **Read the card.** Each station shows the task and the one pass rule for each level.
2. **Self-assess:** "I think I'm at Level ___." Have a go at that level.
3. **Peer verifies** the one objective rule — counts the hits, times the hold, checks the single form cue — and initials.
4. **Teacher sign-off:** the teacher watches your level attempt and gives the final ✔ (a holistic call, plus the safety watch on jump-land). Only then is the level **banked**.
5. **Record** the banked level (1–6) on your sheet → feeds your profile. Re-tested each Combine, so the level is a **trend**, and it carries year to year (G6 → G7 → G8 on one scale).

## To finalise before running
- **Tune the numbers** (distances, counts, hold times) and the **grade bands** against your kids after cycle 1 — bands can be set per skill.
- **Dribble / strike:** confirm the default implement (hand-dribble vs foot; bat vs racket) per unit.
- **Kit per station:** tennis balls, a large ball, **wiffle balls (strike)**, bats + tees, **plenty of cones (gates for strike + kick, slalom for dribble)**, one ~30 cm box, mats, stopwatches/phones, wall/curtain tape for throw/catch zones (strike uses cone gates, no tape).
